## Principle — Featured contest (agreed with owner, 19 Nov 2026)

**Source of truth: the CMS.** Each new contest's facts (year, city, host country,
dates) are stored in the CMS, either by the owner by hand or by Claude on the
owner's command. One contest is marked as the **featured** one and stands out in
the CMS.

**What it drives: the public pages outside the game, in EN and EL.**
- Homepage
- `/eurovision-night`
- The "When is Eurovision {year}?" content
- `/eurovision-predictions`
- Any other public evergreen page that mentions the current contest

**What it does not touch:**
- The rooms (game screens).
- The per-contest pages (e.g. `/eurovision-2026`). These are the finished,
  archived contests and stay as they are.

**SEO metadata follows it too.** Titles, meta descriptions, OG/Twitter tags,
H1s and structured data on those pages are generated from the featured contest.
They are never typed per page.

**When the featured contest changes.** It changes **only when the owner adds a
new contest**. Archiving a contest does not change what the public pages show:
the archived contest stays featured until the next one is added.

**Before the line-up is announced.** The pages show "Not announced yet" /
"Δεν έχουν ακόμα ανακοινωθεί". In place of the line-up they list every country
that has ever competed, from the data, never an empty table.

**Adding the line-up.** The owner enters entries by hand in the new contest in
the CMS, or asks Claude in chat to add what is known. The parser cron remains
the automatic route.

**Evergreen content rule.** Copy must stay true year after year without
rewrites. Counts and ranges come from data, not typed text: "since 1956",
"70 grand finals (1956–2026; 2020 cancelled)", the number of countries. The
first example is the "What is Eurovision trivia?" paragraph.

**No relative-time words.** Words such as "this year's contest", "φέτος",
"tonight", "απόψε", "next year" and "upcoming" go stale as the calendar moves.
Copy names the featured contest explicitly (e.g. "Eurovision 2027 · Burgas"), or
its wording comes from the contest's real state and dates. Examples found:
- `/past-contests`: "This year's contest: Eurovision 2026 →".
- Home kicker: "Tonight live" shown in November.

A first grep found such wording in public copy in `en.json`, `el.json`,
`predictionsText.ts`, `hubCopy.ts`, `archiveCopy.ts` and `archive.tsx`.
`/past-contests` also belongs on the list of affected public pages: its link
points to the featured contest, and its counts ("70 contests") come from the
data. Audit every public
page in both languages for hard-coded years, cities and counts.

**Status (19 Nov 2026): built.**
- The featured contest is the newest one (`siteYear.ts`), and `site.json`
  carries `announced_count`.
- Pages with no line-up say "not announced" and list every country that has
  ever competed.
- The public copy (EN and EL) has no relative-time words and no hand-typed
  counts. There is a new `{stats.countries}` token.

## Principle — What each admin action affects (agreed with owner, 19 Nov 2026)

| Action | Game (rooms) | Public pages | Contest record |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Add new contest** | — | Becomes the featured contest | Created |
| **Finalize** (repeatable while testing) | Scores the rooms; their games end | — | Results final |
| **Archive** | **None** | **None** to the featured pages; the year's page moves to `/past-contests/…` with a 301 (same as 1956–2025) | Locked |

**Game year rule.** New rooms play the newest contest whose entries the parser
has loaded (any stage). Today that is 2026. When the 2027 line-up is parsed and
approved, new rooms switch to 2027 automatically. Existing rooms keep their
year.

Today's code ties both the site year and the game year to the archive state
(`live_contest_year()` and the snapshot's `siteSnapshot` pick). Both must stop
depending on archive.

## Principle — Game data and public data are separate (owner, 19 Nov 2026)

- **Game data:** filled by the parser. It is run many times for testing.
  Within the game it takes only the **grand final** entries (country, artist,
  song) and the **ranking**. It feeds the rooms only and is never published.
- **Public data:** contest facts and line-ups for the public pages. Owned by
  the CMS (owner by hand, or Claude on command), shipped by Publish. The parser
  never writes it.
- **Built in migration 081 (19 Nov 2026):** `game_entries` holds the game
  data and `contest_entries` holds the public data. Finalize no longer
  publishes.
- **Official results reach the public pages through "Copy to public"**: after
  the final Finalize, one button copies the game's final line-up and ranking
  into the CMS data. The owner reviews it, then Publishes. Test runs never
  reach the public side unless the owner presses it.
- **Semi-final results reach the public pages by hand only** (owner, 19 Nov
  2026). After each semi-final the owner either enters the qualifiers and
  points in the CMS, or sends Claude a link in chat. Claude fetches that page
  and updates the contest's semi-final data in the CMS. The parser's `semis`
  data is never copied to the public side. Publish works as for any CMS edit.
  **Built 19 Nov 2026:**
  - The Contests → Edit table shows every semi column: semi, running order,
    place, points and qualified.
  - Claude's write path is `scripts/cms/entries-sql.ts`. It turns an entries
    file into one transaction of `upsert_contest_entry` calls made as the
    admin, with the same checks as the editor. All or nothing, and it never
    publishes.

## Principle — The owner drives the game phases (owner, 19 Nov 2026)

Players join and play (quiz, duels) at any time. The owner moves the game
forward with three separate buttons:

| Button | When the owner presses it | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Open predictions** | when the grand final starts | rooms move to predictions |
| **Close predictions** | when Eurovision's televote opens | rooms move to voting |
| **Finalize** (the existing one on the admin Parser page) | once the final ranking and points are in, from the parser, by hand, or through Claude | scores every room and picks the winners |

**The parse only fetches data.** The final line-up and the results are parsed,
but a parse no longer moves any room.

**Before 079 the code did the opposite:**
- a participants parse turning `done` opened predictions (030, 067);
- a results parse starting closed predictions (032).

Those cascades were already scoped to the job's year (071 E). Only the admin
bulk switch `admin_advance_all_rooms` (the "Room Phases" module) moved rooms of
every year.

**Built in migration 079 (19 Nov 2026):**
- `contests.game_phase` holds each year's phase.
- `set_game_phase(year, phase)` sits behind a **Game phase** card on the Parser
  page, with Open predictions, Close predictions, and Back to before
  predictions for testing.
- The parser triggers no longer move rooms. Finalize still ends that year's
  games and sets `game_phase = final`.
- The host can only start a room from the lobby. The room lands on its year's
  phase.
- `admin_advance_all_rooms` and the Room Phases module are removed.
- `reset_contest` resets `game_phase`.
- The participants parse gate (rule C) is in the edge function.

**Scope: all rooms of the chosen year** (owner's choice). There are no separate
test rooms. Off-season testing on 2025 or 2026 may affect real players' rooms
of that year, and the owner accepts this: parsing already changes the entries
they see.

**Off-season testing must keep working.** The owner tests on 2025 and 2026
with simulated results, through every phase (parse, predictions, voting,
finalize). Nothing may lock the phases outside the season.

## Track 1 — Rethink flows

The full contest year from the owner's seat, one admin step per stage:

1. **New contest:** create the year (city, dates, host). It becomes the site's headline year.
2. **Getting participants:** the parser cron loads the line-up when it is announced. Manual edit stays possible.
3. **Testing / simulating:** test rooms with simulated or manual results, repeatable, invisible to the public site and to the real scoreboards.
4. **Night of Eurovision:** live results from the parser, with fallback to manual entry.
5. **Finalizing results:** score every room from the official ranking. Repeatable until closed.
6. **Closing / ending the contest:** results locked, no new rooms.
7. **Archiving:** the year moves to the archive pages, indexable.
8. **Creating the next contest:** back to step 1.

**Open questions for the owner, asked one at a time:**
- Who tests, and with what results?
- What must stay hidden while testing?
- Is "close" the same moment as "archive"?
- What do players see between contests?

**Deliverable:** a flow spec and a state diagram, then the DB, admin and site changes.

**Parse confirmation (owner request, 19 Nov 2026).** After every participants
or results parse, the owner sees a report of what was found and written: one row
per country with flag, country, artist and song, plus what changed since the
previous run (added, removed, changed). Warnings cover:
- an empty artist or song
- an unknown country
- a count different from what is expected

The parse can find three kinds of rows: past contests' entries, the general
line-up before the semi-finals, and semi-final and final line-ups, each with its
own link. The report shows which source and stage each row came from. It flags
rows that belong to another year or to the wrong stage. It never mixes them into
the featured contest.

**Predictions gate (checked 19 Nov 2026).** Predictions open only after the
participants parse: rooms in `pre_night` flip to `predictions_open` when
`parse_jobs` participants turns `done` (migration 030). A room that leaves the
lobby after that goes straight to predictions (067). The gate is "the parse
ran". It does not check that the parse found a real grand-final line-up. Proof:
on 19 Nov 2026 the 2027 parse finished `done` with 35 countries, all marked
`in_final`, and no artist or song. That is the general list, not the
finalists. No 2027 room exists, because new rooms play 2026 (078), so nothing
broke. Fix: predictions open only on the owner's **Open predictions** button
(see "The owner drives the game phases"). The parse write rule below blocks an
incomplete line-up.

**Write rule (owner's choice C, 19 Nov 2026).** A parse writes straight to
the game data, so the owner can re-run it many times while testing. It is
**blocked, with nothing written**, when it finds any of these:
- an empty artist or song
- an unknown country
- a finalist count outside 20–27

Either way the owner sees the report. Since predictions open only on the
owner's button, the owner checks the report before anyone plays. This replaces
the earlier idea of staging every parse until an Approve.

**Parser page year (fixed 19 Nov 2026).** The admin Parser page used to open
on the newest year in `parse_jobs` (2027 once that contest was created), while
Manual ranking used the game year (2026). The page now opens on the game year
and has a year picker. Parse, manual ranking and Finalize all act on the
picked year.

Superseded: the parse is staged first and goes live only on the owner's **Approve**.
Today the admin shows only "Last run: N rows". A no-write Test exists. The
entries are visible only under Contests → Edit.

**Also in this track:**
- Re-enable the parser cron (`parser-tick`, migration 047) and check it on the first real line-up.
- A night-of runbook.

## Track 3 — CMS redesign

**Decision (owner, 21 Nov 2026): option A.** Our own CMS, inside the existing
`/admin`, built to the level of Storyblok and Sanity. Not WordPress, Kentico,
Sitecore or AEM, and not a hosted or open-source CMS (Storyblok, Sanity,
Payload). Why:
- the CMS and the night-of game controls stay in one admin;
- the publish pipeline and SEO (285 prerendered pages, redirects) stay as they are;
- access stays the 2-email allowlist.

Benchmark features:
- the admin is organized like the site: Header, Footer columns, pages outside the menus;
- every admin screen has its own URL, and every page links to its public URL;
- click-to-edit on the preview;
- autosave;
- per-page publish with a diff against live;
- version history and restore;
- EN/EL side by side with missing-translation flags;
- ⌘K search;
- in-CMS SEO checks.

Editors: the owner and Elena both write and both publish, with no review step.
The CMS must handle two people editing at once. Today a conflict is refused on
save; target: show who is on which page.

Timing: stays in its Track 3 slot, after the Track 2 redesign (owner, 21 Nov 2026).

Access stays as it is:
- login plus the allowlist enforced in every RPC;
- noindex meta, the `X-Robots-Tag` header and the robots.txt Disallow;
- no public links to `/admin`.

Found 21 Nov 2026:
- the footer and header text lives in code and i18n files, not in the CMS;
- `/eurovision-party` is in no menu;
- `/rules` is only in the header.

1. **Audit:** the owner lists what is painful (editing, previews, publish, media, contests).
2. **Spec:** information architecture and editing model, built on the Track 2 design system.
3. **Build and migrate:** content stays where it is (the same JSON and publish pipeline), so nothing public breaks.
