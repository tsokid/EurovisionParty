## Principle — Featured contest (agreed with owner, 19 Nov 2026)

**Source of truth: the CMS.** Each new contest's facts (year, city, host country,
dates) are stored in the CMS, either by the owner by hand or by Claude on the
owner's command. One contest is marked as the **featured** one and stands out in
the CMS.

**What it drives: the public pages outside the game, in EN and EL.**
- Homepage
- `/eurovision-night`
- The "When is Eurovision {year}?" content
- `/eurovision-predictions`
- Any other public evergreen page that mentions the current contest

**What it does not touch:**
- The rooms (game screens).
- The per-contest pages (e.g. `/eurovision-2026`). These are the finished,
  archived contests and stay as they are.

**SEO metadata follows it too.** Titles, meta descriptions, OG/Twitter tags,
H1s and structured data on those pages are generated from the featured contest.
They are never typed per page.

**When the featured contest changes.** It changes **only when the owner adds a
new contest**. Archiving a contest does not change what the public pages show:
the archived contest stays featured until the next one is added.

**Before the line-up is announced.** The pages show "Not announced yet" /
"Δεν έχουν ακόμα ανακοινωθεί". In place of the line-up they list every country
that has ever competed, from the data, never an empty table.

**Adding the line-up.** The owner enters entries by hand in the new contest in
the CMS, or asks Claude in chat to add what is known. The parser cron remains
the automatic route.

**Evergreen content rule.** Copy must stay true year after year without
rewrites. Counts and ranges come from data, not typed text: "since 1956",
"70 grand finals (1956–2026; 2020 cancelled)", the number of countries. The
first example is the "What is Eurovision trivia?" paragraph.

**No relative-time words.** Words such as "this year's contest", "φέτος",
"tonight", "απόψε", "next year" and "upcoming" go stale as the calendar moves.
Copy names the featured contest explicitly (e.g. "Eurovision 2027 · Burgas"), or
its wording comes from the contest's real state and dates. Examples found:
- `/past-contests`: "This year's contest: Eurovision 2026 →".
- Home kicker: "Tonight live" shown in November.

A first grep found such wording in public copy in `en.json`, `el.json`,
`predictionsText.ts`, `hubCopy.ts`, `archiveCopy.ts` and `archive.tsx`.
`/past-contests` also belongs on the list of affected public pages: its link
points to the featured contest, and its counts ("70 contests") come from the
data. Audit every public
page in both languages for hard-coded years, cities and counts.

**Status:** kept in the plan only; nothing is built until the owner says go.

## Principle — What each admin action affects (agreed with owner, 19 Nov 2026)

| Action | Game (rooms) | Public pages | Contest record |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Add new contest** | — | Becomes the featured contest | Created |
| **Finalize** (repeatable while testing) | Scores the rooms; their games end | — | Results final |
| **Archive** | **None** | **None** to the featured pages; the year's page moves to `/past-contests/…` with a 301 (same as 1956–2025) | Locked |

**Game year rule.** New rooms play the newest contest whose entries the parser
has loaded (any stage). Today that is 2026. When the 2027 line-up is parsed and
approved, new rooms switch to 2027 automatically. Existing rooms keep their
year.

Today's code ties both the site year and the game year to the archive state
(`live_contest_year()` and the snapshot's `siteSnapshot` pick). Both must stop
depending on archive.

## Track 1 — Rethink flows

The full contest year from the owner's seat, one admin step per stage:

1. **New contest:** create the year (city, dates, host). It becomes the site's headline year.
2. **Getting participants:** the parser cron loads the line-up when it is announced. Manual edit stays possible.
3. **Testing / simulating:** test rooms with simulated or manual results, repeatable, invisible to the public site and to the real scoreboards.
4. **Night of Eurovision:** live results from the parser, with fallback to manual entry.
5. **Finalizing results:** score every room from the official ranking. Repeatable until closed.
6. **Closing / ending the contest:** results locked, no new rooms.
7. **Archiving:** the year moves to the archive pages, indexable.
8. **Creating the next contest:** back to step 1.

**Open questions for the owner, asked one at a time:**
- Who tests, and with what results?
- What must stay hidden while testing?
- Is "close" the same moment as "archive"?
- What do players see between contests?

**Deliverable:** a flow spec and a state diagram, then the DB, admin and site changes.

**Parse confirmation (owner request, 19 Nov 2026).** After every participants
or results parse, the owner sees a report of what was found and written: one row
per country with flag, country, artist and song, plus what changed since the
previous run (added, removed, changed). Warnings cover:
- an empty artist or song
- an unknown country
- a count different from what is expected

The parse can find three kinds of rows: past contests' entries, the general
line-up before the semi-finals, and semi-final and final line-ups, each with its
own link. The report shows which source and stage each row came from. It flags
rows that belong to another year or to the wrong stage. It never mixes them into
the featured contest.

The parse is staged first and goes live only on the owner's **Approve**.
Today the admin shows only "Last run: N rows". A no-write Test exists. The
entries are visible only under Contests → Edit.

**Also in this track:**
- Re-enable the parser cron (`parser-tick`, migration 047) and check it on the first real line-up.
- A night-of runbook.

## Track 2 — Web app redesign (mobile first)

1. **Audit:** every screen on a phone (home, create/join, lobby, predictions, live voting, duels, quiz, results, winners), plus desktop.
2. **Direction:** look and feel options with mockups. The owner picks one. Fonts stay as they are (existing constraint).
3. **Design system:** colours, type scale, spacing, components, motion, all in code.
4. **Screens:** rebuilt one flow at a time behind the same routes. URLs, titles and meta are unchanged, so the SEO is untouched.
5. **Desktop:** layouts that use the width, not stretched phone screens.
6. **Quality:** accessibility, performance (Core Web Vitals) and a real-device check.

---

## Track 3 — CMS redesign

1. **Audit:** the owner lists what is painful (editing, previews, publish, media, contests).
2. **Spec:** information architecture and editing model, built on the Track 2 design system.
3. **Build and migrate:** content stays where it is (the same JSON and publish pipeline), so nothing public breaks.

## Track 4 — Season hardening (Apr → May 2027)

- Two dress rehearsals: a full simulated night with friends, from test rooms through finalize to the reset.
- A load check on rooms, live updates and the parser.
- Night of 15 May: owner runbook, live monitoring, manual fallback ready.
- After the night: finalize, close and archive, then create 2028.

## SEO guardrails (every track)

- No URL changes without a 301.
- Titles and meta are generated from data, never typed per page.
- Frozen-title tests and the prerender, sitemap and robots checks pass on every release.
- `/admin` stays noindex and hidden.
